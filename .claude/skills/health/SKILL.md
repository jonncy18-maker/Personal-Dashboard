## Health › Diet

**Scoped and built 2026-09-16 (see that ROADMAP.md entry for the full reasoning). The shape is inverted from every other domain here: the app is the database, the calculation and the presentation layer, and Claude over MCP is the primary capture path.** That is a cost decision, not a novelty one — food photos billed to `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` are image tokens charged per meal forever (~1,400 vision calls a year at four meals a day), while the same work in a flat-rate Claude surface costs nothing marginal. For a single-user app where John is the only person logging, moving capture out of the app is correct. The app keeps full CRUD as the fallback so a broken connector never costs a week of logging. `/health` redirects to `/health/diet`; the segment exists so a second tab later is a new file, not a route rename touching the sidebar, the Home card and the PWA start URL. There is **no training log** — John records lifts as "Other workout" on his Samsung watch and will not retype them; if watch data is ever read, "Other workout" maps to weights.

**Three source tiers on every intake row, and the tilde is the whole point.** `source` is required at every boundary and **never defaulted or inferred** — a silent default quietly promotes a guess to a transcribed fact. `label` = read off a nutrition panel or a posted menu (transcription; the number exists in the world). `recall` = a branded item recalled from model memory — a real published figure, possibly stale, delivered with identical confidence either way. `estimated` = from pixels or a generic description; no authoritative number exists at all. **Any single non-`label` entry makes the day's total an estimate, rendered with a leading `~`.** Same number, weaker claim. `dayTotals()` in `lib/health.js` owns that rule; never re-derive it in a component. Editing a row's calories by hand re-tiers a `label` down to `estimated` (`app/api/health/intake/[id]`) — a hand-typed number has not earned the label badge. A third provenance axis, `logged_via` (`app` | `mcp`), records the capture path and is displayed, never used in math.

**The daily target is derived-with-provenance, never a bare number.** Mifflin–St Jeor over fields John maintains, and the UI always shows the formula name and its inputs alongside the figure — the same treatment Maintenance gives interval provenance. This is what makes a computed health target defensible under CLAUDE.md §7.3: it is a third category next to "real data" and "hardcoded constant". **`manual_target_cal` wins before anything else is computed** — a doctor's number stops the formula entirely rather than being blended with it. `computeTarget()` returns `null` plus a `missing[]` list rather than a partial answer when an input is absent; a page with no body stats shows "no target yet", never a plausible default. **Ethnicity is not an input to any standard energy equation** and must not be added as a field. The floor is `floor_pct` of maintenance (or a flat `manual_floor_cal`), never a constant in code.

**Clamp and slip: the target stops at the floor and the DATE moves.** When the deficit a goal date demands would push the target below the safe floor, `computeTarget()` clamps to the floor, sets `clamped`, and reports a real `projectedDate` computed from the achievable deficit. The app never bends the target to hit a date it cannot safely hit, and never displays an unsafe figure because the arithmetic produced one. A goal already met, or a date already passed, holds at maintenance rather than emitting a negative deficit.

**Weight is a dated log, gap-tolerant by design, and it is the target's weight input.** One reading per calendar day — a second POST for that date corrects it rather than appending, which is what keeps "latest reading" unambiguous. John weighs sporadically (weekly as intent, his own words: "more sporadic"), so **no streak counter, no "you missed this week", and never interpolation across a gap.** The trend chart positions X by real elapsed days, not by array index — that is precisely what makes a three-week gap look like a gap. A stale reading still feeds the target but is **labelled with its age** wherever the target appears, rather than silently presented as current.

**The completeness signal is load-bearing, not decoration.** "Calories left today" cannot distinguish _hasn't eaten_ from _hasn't logged_, and at 9pm the second one renders as a triumph. Unlogged food is the one structural way this domain can lie — missing entries do not look like gaps, they look like success. So the figure **never appears without** `Nothing logged today` / `N of 4 meals logged` beside it, on the page hero and on the Home card alike. Do not "simplify" that line away.

**MCP auth is a bearer token that fails closed.** `HEALTH_MCP_TOKEN`, checked in `app/api/mcp/health/route.js` — the same shape as the `CRON_SECRET` gate on `/api/trip-scan`, and a shared secret on a machine endpoint rather than a user session layer, so CLAUDE.md §7.1 ("no auth — don't add it back") is intact: still no login, no user table, no cookie. **Unlike the cron gate, an unset token returns 503 rather than running unauthenticated** — a stray GET on the cron route only costs a scan, whereas these tools write to the database. Transport is hand-rolled JSON-RPC 2.0 (`initialize` / `tools/list` / `tools/call`) rather than `@modelcontextprotocol/sdk`, keeping the dependency surface as lean as the rest of the stack. The `log_food` tool description tells the model in so many words not to claim `label` for a number it produced itself; keep that instruction if the tool is edited.

**Five tools now, not three (`update_intake_entry`/`delete_intake_entry` added 2026-09-17).** John hit a real gap reviewing the app-wide MCP PR: he could log food but not fix a typo or a wrong calorie figure without deleting and re-adding — matching the same gap the `/health/diet` page had until that same session. `update_intake_entry` mirrors `app/api/health/intake/[id]`'s PATCH exactly, including the re-tier-a-hand-edited-label rule (a calorie change with no explicit new `source` silently downgrades a `label` row to `estimated`), so the honesty mechanism holds regardless of which of the three surfaces (page, Health MCP, app-wide MCP) touched the row.

**Steps joined weight on the same dated row, not a new table (migration 030, same session).** `health_weight_readings` gained a nullable `steps` column — `weight_lb` had to stop being `NOT NULL` for this, since a steps-only day is a real row with no weight in it. `log_steps` is a sixth tool, upserting by date exactly like `log_weight` (a second log for a date already logged corrects it, never appends). This mattered enough to touch three separate "latest weight" queries (`app/api/health/route.js`, `app/api/home-summary/route.js`, `lib/health-mcp-tools.js`'s `readDay`) that all assumed `weight_lb` was never null — each now filters `WHERE weight_lb IS NOT NULL` before taking the latest row, or a steps-only day would have silently broken the target's weight input. `todaySteps` (the API) / `steps` (the MCP tools) is deliberately NOT the same lookup as latest-weight: steps are a per-day fact with no forward-fill, so a day with no steps row reads as "unknown," never "assume yesterday's count."

**The watch-fed activity multiplier was built 2026-09-17 (migration 031), moving it off the deferred list below.** `health_profile.activity_source` (`'manual'` default, or `'steps_trailing'`) and `activity_trailing_days` (default 14) let John opt a profile into deriving the Mifflin–St Jeor activity multiplier from a trailing average of logged steps instead of typing a fixed number. `lib/health.js` maps a trailing average onto the same five standard categories (sedentary 1.2 through extra-active 1.9, banded at 5000/7500/10000/12500 steps/day) via `stepsToActivityMultiplier()`; `trailingStepsAverage()` computes the window average **gap-tolerant and never zero-filled** — a day with no steps row is excluded from the average entirely, not counted as zero. **The core safeguard from the original 2026-09-16 scoping decision holds exactly as scoped: the multiplier only ever moves from *trailing*, *historical* data — never from the current day's own step count — so there is no intraday calorie credit.** A wearable crediting today's steps back into today's target on top of a multiplier that already assumes elevated activity would double-count the same movement, compounding wearables' well-documented 20–40% overestimate of active burn. A minimum-data gate (half the window must have logged days) keeps the derived multiplier from being computed off a sparse trailing window; below that threshold `computeTarget()` reports `activityProvenance: 'steps_trailing_pending'` and falls back to the profile's manual `activity_multiplier` rather than silently guessing — the UI states outright how many more logged days are needed, per the same "never silently substitute a plausible default" rule as everywhere else in this domain. `activity_multiplier` itself keeps working exactly as before as both the manual value and the pending-fallback value — existing `'manual'` profiles see zero behavior change. The `/health/diet` profile form now has the `manual` / `steps_trailing` toggle and the window-size field, and the provenance line shows the resolved multiplier, its category label, and the trailing average feeding it (or the "not enough step data yet" honesty message).

All six tools (`get_day`/`log_food`/`log_weight`/`log_steps`/`update_intake_entry`/`delete_intake_entry`) live in `lib/health-mcp-tools.js`, shared verbatim by `app/api/mcp/health` and the app-wide server — see the `assistant` skill for how the in-app Assistant also picked these up the same session.

**Protein/carbs/fat and saved favorite meals, built 2026-09-18 (migration 032).** Protein was the strongest item on the deferred list below — "at 122 lb lifting in a deficit, protein is what decides fat versus muscle" — and John logs almost entirely through the MCP connector, which was already estimating macros with nothing to write them to. `protein_g`/`carbs_g`/`fat_g` on `health_intake_entries` are nullable, same reasoning as `steps`: an existing or hand-typed row may simply not have them, and defaulting to 0 would misrepresent "not logged" as "zero grams." `dayTotals()` in `lib/health.js` sums only the entries that actually carry a macro and reports a `*Complete` flag per macro (every entry this day has it) — a partial sum is never allowed to read as the day's whole figure; the page shows a `~` on an incomplete macro total the same way it does on estimated calories. `log_food`/`update_intake_entry` (both MCP tools) grew matching optional fields, with the same "never zero-fill, never guess to fill the field" instruction as `source`.

**`health_favorite_meals` is a template table, not a log.** John eats the same breakfast most days, so re-typing description/calories/macros every time was pure friction. A favorite (name, optional default meal, description, calories, macros, its own `source`) is saved once; logging it (`POST /api/health/favorites/[id]/log`, or the `log_favorite_meal` MCP tool) copies its fields into a fresh, independent `health_intake_entries` row dated today or whatever date is asked for — editing or deleting that entry afterward never touches the template. `source` on a favorite is not inherited automatically into some higher trust tier by being reused; it's whatever tier the underlying number actually earns, same as any other entry. Four new MCP tools (`list_favorite_meals`/`save_favorite_meal`/`log_favorite_meal`/`delete_favorite_meal`) round the tool count to ten, all still in `lib/health-mcp-tools.js` and picked up by both MCP servers and the in-app Assistant automatically (they all consume `HEALTH_TOOLS`/`isHealthTool` directly — no separate allowlist edit needed). The `/health/diet` page got a matching Favorites card (list, one-tap log with a meal picker for mealless favorites, a "New favorite" form, delete) as the manual-path fallback.

**`health_recommended_meals` is a third, separate table from favorites — Claude-curated nudges toward a healthier baseline, built 2026-09-18 (migration 033).** Do not conflate this with `health_favorite_meals`: favorites are things John already eats and saves himself; recommendations are suggestions *to* him, almost always Claude's idea in the first place. Two horizons: `'today'` reacts to a specific day's remaining calories/macros (`for_date` required, matches the DB CHECK), `'ongoing'` is a standing habit-level suggestion with no expiry (`for_date` must be null — same CHECK). Meal-shaped fields (`meal`/`calories`/`protein_g`/`carbs_g`/`fat_g`) are all nullable, since an `'ongoing'` suggestion can be pure guidance text ("add more fiber at breakfast") with no specific food behind it — `log_recommended_meal` refuses to fire on one with no `calories`. **No `source` tier on the recommendation itself** — it isn't logged food yet, so the label/recall/estimated honesty system doesn't apply; logging one (`POST /api/health/recommendations/[id]/log` or the MCP tool) creates an independent `health_intake_entries` row tiered `'estimated'` unconditionally (it's always Claude's own number, never a transcribed label).

**Every write tool here carries an explicit confirm-first instruction, because Claude has full CRUD control by design.** John's own words scoping this: "Claude should have full control of the list but should confirm with me first... I'm thinking these recommendations will come from Claude chat, and then I'll say, go ahead and push to the app." There is no in-app preview screen for this (unlike a screenshot import) — **the chat conversation itself is the preview/confirm step**, so `add_recommended_meal`/`update_recommended_meal` both say in so many words never to call without John having just agreed in that same conversation. `delete_recommended_meal` keeps the same "destructive, confirm first" wording used everywhere else. Five new MCP tools (`list_recommended_meals`/`add_recommended_meal`/`update_recommended_meal`/`delete_recommended_meal`/`log_recommended_meal`) bring the health tool count to fifteen. The `/health/diet` page also got a full manual fallback — a Recommended card with add/log/delete, separate from Favorites — since John asked for that explicitly rather than making this Claude-only.

**`/health/diet` was redesigned around a chronological timeline, 2026-09-18.** John rejected the original card-grid hero (a dark full-width gradient banner) and the four always-visible meal buckets as cluttered, and picked a timeline mockup over four others after a round of Design-canvas mockups. The layout is now sidebar (day's numbers, pinned) + main panel (the day's log): the sidebar holds a light `.card` with `BudgetRing` (recolored for a light background — the old ring was styled for the dark hero it no longer sits on) plus `MacroBar` (a proportional protein/carbs/fat bar that **only renders when all three are known** — built from a partial set it would imply a split that was never measured; the legend numbers always show whatever exists) and the Weight & Steps card unchanged in behavior. The main panel drops the four fixed meal-bucket sections (each always rendering a header + "+ Log" button even at zero entries) for one `sortedEntries` timeline ordered by `created_at`, each row tagged by a per-meal color (`MEAL_INFO`) instead of a repeated text label. **A logged time only ever renders on `isToday`** — `created_at` is when a row was written, not when the meal happened, so showing a clock time on a backfilled past day would silently claim a precision the data doesn't have; past days show the meal tag with no time. Favorites and Recommended merge into one "Meal Library": a `chipRow` of one-tap quick-add chips (favorites always, recommendations only when they carry a `calories` figure — a pure habit suggestion has nothing to log) that infers a meal from time-of-day (`inferMealFromNow()`) when the source has no default, plus a "Manage" toggle that reveals the original `FavoritesCard`/`RecommendationsCard` (unchanged, full CRUD) for anything a chip can't do. The Mifflin–St Jeor formula text collapses behind a "Formula & activity" toggle **only** when there's an actual formula to hide — a manual override or a missing-inputs state always shows its message outright, since those are actionable, not decorative detail. No API or schema changes — this was presentation-only; `app/api/health/route.js` already returned everything the new layout needed.

**Trailing net calorie surplus/deficit, added 2026-09-18 as a sidebar card next to the timeline redesign.** `GET /api/health/net?range=week|month|ytd` sums `consumed - target` across every day in the window that actually has logged intake — **a day with nothing logged is excluded from the sum entirely, never counted as a full deficit**, same "didn't log ≠ ate nothing" principle as `dayTotals()`'s completeness signal, just applied across days instead of within one. Computing this means recomputing `computeTarget()` for every day in range (weight forward-filled as of that day, trailing-steps multiplier windowed from that day) since the target itself can drift day to day — this is real work, which is why it's a separate lazy-fetched card (`NetCaloriesCard`, its own `useResource` call) rather than folded into the main `/api/health` response every page load pays for. The response reports `daysLogged` against `daysInRange` so a 2-of-30-days figure never reads as a confident month total, and `estimated` bubbles up if any counted day's total included a non-`label` entry. `week`/`month` are trailing windows (last 7 / last 30 days including today), not calendar weeks/months; `ytd` is calendar-year-to-date.

**The page itself (`/health/diet`) gained date navigation and a Weight/Steps Today-vs-Trend split, same session.** `‹`/`›` arrows browse any past day (next-day is disabled past today — no future dates); the GET already accepted a `date` query param, so this was a client-only addition, and Add/Edit/Delete/weight/steps all now target whatever date is being viewed, not always "today." The Weight card's "Trend" tab filters the same trend array (its `TREND_DAYS` cap raised from 90 to 3650 in `app/api/health/route.js` once the client started slicing arbitrary windows client-side) into This Month / Year to Date / a custom two-date range — no new endpoint, just client-side filtering on bare `'YYYY-MM-DD'` string comparison (safe because those sort correctly as strings, same reasoning as everywhere else dates appear in this app).

**A thin OAuth wrapper exists on top of that same token, added 2026-09-17, only for claude.ai's hosted connector.** claude.ai's "Add custom connector" UI requires OAuth 2.1 + dynamic client registration to reach a remote MCP server at all — it can't be pointed at a bare bearer header, and getting this working live took several iterations (see ROADMAP.md's run of 2026-09-17 entries for the real dead ends: a Vercel-project-level login wall blocking every automated caller including this OAuth flow, then a bypass-secret attempt that failed because RFC 9728 forbids a query string on the issuer field — the wall was ultimately turned off project-wide as the actual fix). `app/api/mcp/health/{register,authorize,token}/route.js` are now thin wrappers over `lib/mcp-server.js`'s shared route-handler factories (`createMcpHandler`, `createAuthorizeHandlers`, `createTokenHandler`, `registerHandler`), and the `.well-known` metadata routes wrap `lib/mcp-oauth.js`'s `protectedResourceMetadata`/`authorizationServerMetadata` — both shared verbatim with the app-wide server at `/api/mcp/app` (see the `assistant` skill), not reimplemented. Migration 029's `health_mcp_auth_codes` table (despite its health-flavored name) holds short-lived, single-use authorization codes for **every** MCP server this app exposes — a code is opaque and single-use regardless of which resource issued it, so sharing the one table costs nothing. **This is not a second credential.** `/authorize` is a one-field HTML form asking for the same `HEALTH_MCP_TOKEN`; the access/refresh token the flow ultimately returns IS that token, so `/api/mcp/health`'s own bearer check never changes. Claude Code's MCP config still takes the bearer header directly and can skip all of this — the OAuth wrapper is only needed for claude.ai's chat app.

**Vegetable servings, built 2026-09-23 (migration 035) — the one deliberate `NOT NULL DEFAULT 0` field.** `veggie_servings` on `health_intake_entries` (and on `health_favorite_meals`, copied on log) counts servings at ≈1 cup raw / ½ cup cooked. Unlike the macros, a missing value is 0, not null, because it only feeds a "did the day reach its minimum" check: an unassessed 0 can only under-count (reads *not met*), never fabricate a success. Do not "fix" it to nullable, and do not copy this default onto the macros — a zero there has no safe direction. The daily baseline is `health_profile.veggie_target_servings` (default 2), never a constant in code; `veggieProgress()` in `lib/health.js` owns the met/not-met comparison and returns `met: null` without a target. `get_day` reports `veggie_servings_total`/`veggie_target_servings`/`veggie_target_met`. Fiber grams were deliberately not built.

**"Today" is the device's date, never the server's (2026-09-26, Codex audit F08, John's call).** Vercel runs in UTC, so any server-side default (a log with no date, `get_day` with no date, the trailing-net window, the Home card) used to read a day ahead every Eastern evening. Server code must call `deviceToday()` from `lib/device-time.js`, never bare `todayYMD()`: it uses the timezone the browser reports (`components/DeviceTimezone.jsx` → `/api/device-timezone`, kept in `app_flags` as `device_timezone`), i.e. the zone of the device John most recently opened the app on, so MCP callers with no browser in the loop follow him when he travels. The in-app Assistant also sends its own device's zone with each message. Never recorded, or unreadable → the server clock, the old behavior. Explicit dates always win. In the browser, plain `todayYMD()` is already the device's date.

**Water, built 2026-09-23 (migration 036) — its own log, never an intake row.** `health_water_entries` holds one row per drink (US fl oz); the day's figure is a SUM, so `log_water` must be called per drink ("I just drank an 8 oz cup" → amount 8), never with a running total. It is deliberately not a 0-calorie `health_intake_entries` row: that would count toward "N of 4 meals logged", the completeness signal above, and make an unlogged day look more logged than it is. Units: `toWaterOunces()` in `lib/health.js` converts `oz`/`cup` (the 8 fl oz US cup)/`ml` on the way in. The goal, `health_profile.water_target_oz`, is **nullable with no default**, unlike `veggie_target_servings`: John named a veggie baseline but no water goal, so with none set `waterProgress()` reports `met: null` and the page shows the total with no bar or verdict. Don't seed a "64 oz" default. `get_day` returns `water_oz_total`/`water_cups_total`/`water_target_oz`/`water_target_met`/`water_entries`; `delete_water_entry` is confirm-first like every delete. A logged time shows only on today, same rule as the food timeline.

**Drinks logged as food count toward water (migration 037).** `fluid_oz` (nullable; NULL = not a drink) on `health_intake_entries` and `health_favorite_meals` is the liquid a drink is made with: a protein shake made with 12 oz of milk or water → 12. It is counted **in full**, not scaled by a guessed water-content fraction (John asked; decided 2026-09-23): milk, coffee and tea are mostly water, and a per-drink percentage would add a fabricated number to move the total by ~10%. Powders and solids don't count; the tool description says to omit it for alcohol. `waterProgress(entries, targetOz, foodEntries)` returns plain water and drink fluid **separately** (`plainOunces`/`fromDrinksOunces`, `get_day`'s `water_plain_oz`/`water_from_drinks_oz`), and the page prints the split whenever drinks contributed, so the estimated share of the total is always visible. Favorites carry it, so the usual shake counts every time it's logged.

**Deferred to v2, deliberately — not oversights.** Push-notification nudges; and **observed-maintenance calibration** — deriving true maintenance from logged intake against a flat weight trend, which beats Mifflin–St Jeor's ~±10% individual error because it is a direct observation of one person rather than a population regression. That one is deferred only because it cannot work on day one; the v1 obligation to it is that dated intake and weight rows make it a pure add-on, needing no new tables and no backfill. (Protein/macros and the watch-fed activity multiplier that used to be on this list were built 2026-09-18 and 2026-09-17 respectively — see above.)
